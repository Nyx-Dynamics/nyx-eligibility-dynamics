Temporary Loss of Eligibility Cancels Exactly in Cross-Sectional HIV Incidence Estimation

BACKGROUND: Cross-sectional recency assays increasingly reconstruct counterfactual placebo incidence for HIV prevention trials, where placebo arms are seldom ethical. They condition on eligibility at survey, yet eligibility often changes between acquisition and sampling through incarceration, migration, hospitalization, or disengagement from care. Existing frameworks assume the moving fraction is small rather than characterizing what movement does. Whether temporary loss biases incidence, and in which direction, is unestablished.

METHODS: We refined the Gao-Bannick eligibility indicator A(t) as the observable state of a finite-state Markov process admitting temporarily unobservable states and an absorbing state. We derived the expected recent-infection count allowing acquisition in every living state, not only while observable, and obtained the estimator's probability limit as an integral against a historical observability weight w(u). We composed this process with the Pan-Bannick-Gao model of survey attendance and prior-testing exclusion. Results were verified by three implementations sharing no code: matrix exponential, adaptive quadrature, and a generative individual-level simulator.

RESULTS: Temporary eligibility loss did not itself bias estimation. Under five conditions, stable living-state composition, a demographically stationary observable susceptible pool, state-invariant acquisition hazard, infection-independent movement, and no absorbing loss, losses and returns cancelled exactly: lambda-hat/lambda-E = 1 to machine precision across nine occupancy-by-sojourn combinations, independently of the recency function, and at occupancies to 15%. Cancellation held within each stationary stratum and therefore under arbitrary mixtures of movement propensity, so recurrent movement concentrated in a high-propensity subgroup did not break it. Bias required a specific symmetry failure. Absorbing mortality at 0.040/year attenuated estimates by 2.1% (0.979); moving the composed zero-bias boundary above unity required 0.085/year, 2.1-fold sourced rates. Acquisition while unobservable attenuated or inflated estimates depending on relative hazard (0.944 at eta=0, 1.007 at eta=1.8). Setting w(u)=1 recovered the published Pan-Bannick-Gao limiting expression exactly.

CONCLUSIONS: Eligibility loss should not be treated as inherently biasing. Transient, bidirectional movement cancels under identifiable symmetry conditions; correction is warranted only where a named condition fails. Absorbing and temporary loss are therefore not interchangeable, and the parameter governing sensitivity is the state-specific acquisition hazard, not the occupancy of unobservable states. Occupancy alone cannot establish that an estimate is biased.
