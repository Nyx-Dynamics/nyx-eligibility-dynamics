## Title

**Submit:** *Temporary Loss of Eligibility Cancels Exactly in Cross-Sectional HIV Incidence Estimation*

Eleven words. Three changes from the draft title:

- **"Temporary" for "Transient."** The whole point is a contrast with *absorbing* loss, and
  "temporary" carries that to a clinical reader without a definition. "Transient" also has a
  competing technical sense in Markov theory (a transient *state*), which is not what is meant.
- **"Cancels" for "Can cancel."** "Can" invites the reader to file this as a theoretical
  possibility. The result is an equality under stated conditions, and the conditions are named
  in the first line of Results. Assertive titles also survive CROI's programme listing better.
- Dropped "Bias" phrasing entirely so the title states the finding, not the topic.

Two alternates, if a co-author objects to an assertive title:

| | Title | Note |
|---|---|---|
| B | Eligibility Dynamics in Cross-Sectional HIV Incidence Estimation: Exact Cancellation and Its Failure Modes | Neutral, more conventional; buries the finding after a colon |
| C | When Does Leaving the Observable Population Bias HIV Incidence Estimates? | Question form; good for a poster walk-up, weaker in a citation |

Avoid the original's "Can Cancel Exactly" — reviewers read hedged titles as preliminary work.

## Figure

**Submit `outputs/figures/croi_falsification.pdf`** (vector; `.png` at 600 dpi alongside).
Caption in `FIGURE_CAPTION.txt`, 406 words, regenerated by the same script that draws the
figure so the two cannot drift apart. Two panels:

- **A.** Census sampling. λ̂/λ_E by condition: the five-condition case pinned at 1, then each
  symmetry failure one at a time. Open circles analytic, red points Monte Carlo. A narrow
  attached strip shows the same seven comparisons **studentised**, band at ±2 SE — because at
  the value scale the 95% intervals are ~0.3% of the axis and disappear under the markers. A
  figure whose claim is agreement has to show agreement at the resolution it was tested at,
  not at one where any result would look fine.
- **B.** Composed with the Pan screening and prior-testing stages. Limiting estimation error
  against the attendance ratio r, for w ≡ 1 and with dynamics; triangles mark the predicted
  zero-bias boundary, which the simulator reproduces without being given it.

All 19 comparisons agree within |t| = 2.20 on 23 df. Regenerate with `make croi-figure`
(~20 min, 5.7e9 draws); restyle without re-simulating via
`python3 falsification_figure.py --redraw`, which rebuilds from the committed tables.

This is the falsification, not a sensitivity surface: it shows that two independent
implementations agree, that the cancellation is exact rather than approximate, and that each
named mechanism moves the estimate in the predicted direction. The abstract's credibility claim
is "three implementations sharing no code," and this is the only figure that makes that visible.

**Plot text is deliberately minimal**, per the brief: no in-plot title, no annotations, no
inline numbers. Only tick labels (bare parameter values), two axis labels, and a shared legend
below both panels. Everything interpretive — mechanism names, the five conditions, sojourn
lengths, θ, c, MDRI — is in the caption.

### Rejected alternatives

**The (η_J, η_P) contour surface.** The best figure in the project and the wrong one here. It is
a *sensitivity* object about a parameter that has never been measured, so in a 348-word abstract
headlining an exact equality it moves attention from the theorem to "what is η in your
population?" — where the honest answer is that no contemporary PWID-specific estimate exists.
Fine in discussion, poor in review. It also carries the jail/prison site work, held out of v1.
Build it for the poster, where there is room to say what η is and is not.

**A cancellation schematic.** A flow diagram of E leaving to O and returning restates one
sentence of Results. Schematics earn space when a mechanism is hard to hold in words.

Two wording notes on the numbers:

- **"to machine precision," not "2.2e-16."** The recorded 2.22e-16 was a 1201-point grid; at

  all-cause adult PWID mortality, 0.040/yr; state it in the poster, not the abstract.

## Claims deliberately NOT made

Listed so nothing gets added back under deadline pressure.

- No claim that any specific published trial estimate is biased, or by how much. The abstract
  says when correction is and is not warranted.
- No claim about eta's value in any real population. It is a free parameter shown in both
  directions.
- No claim that the cancellation requires reversible or detailed-balanced movement. It needs
  stationarity only, and saying otherwise understates the result.
- No claim of exactness for the boundary beyond the Poisson inter-test case, where it holds
  exactly. Under a uniform inter-test process invariance is approximate (<=0.6%).
